Procedure -
* To properly dehydrate yellow onions so they don't spoil or burn, use the following temperature and timing guidelines:

    For a Food Dehydrator (Highly Recommended)
       • Temperature: Set your dehydrator to 52°C to 57°C (125°F to 135°F).
       • Timing: It will take anywhere from 6 to 12 hours.
    For a Conventional Kitchen Oven
        • Temperature: Set your oven to its lowest possible setting, ideally 60°C to 75°C (140°F to 170°F). Prop the oven door open slightly with a wooden spoon to let moisture escape.
        • Timing: It will take 3 to 6 hours. You must watch them closely so they do not bake or scorch.

    3 Rules for the Perfect Dried Onion
        1. Slice Evenly: Slice your onions thin and completely uniform—about 3mm to 6mm (1/8 to 1/4 inch) thick. If some pieces are thick and others are thin, the thin ones will burn while the thick ones stay damp.
        2. The "Snapping" Test: The onions are only fully done when they are completely brittle and crisp. If you bend a piece, it should snap like a potato chip. If it feels leathery, bendy, or soft, it still has moisture and will mold in your jar. Let them dry longer.
        3. Condition the Jars: After drying, put the cooled onions in a glass jar for a week. Shake the jar daily. If you see any condensation or fogging on the glass, the onions aren't dry enough—put them back in the dehydrator immediately.

    * The temperature and timing work for any quantity of onions, because dehydration time depends on the thickness of the slices and airflow, not the total weight.

    * The Golden Rule: Single Layer Only -
        Never pile the onions on top of each other. They must sit in a single, flat layer with space around them so the warm air can evaporate the moisture. Overcrowding the trays will trap moisture, causing the onions to sweat and steam instead of drying out, which drastically increases the time and can cause them to spoil.

    * Why 3mm to 6mm -
        • Why this thickness works best: It strikes the perfect balance. If you cut them thinner than 3mm, they become paper-thin, easily burn, and stick aggressively to your dehydrator trays. If you cut them thicker than 6mm, the outside dries out while the inside stays damp, making the dehydration process take significantly longer.
